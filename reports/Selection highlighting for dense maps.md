# Mark every light, never the group

Every product family surveyed marks selection on each item and never draws a hull around a selected subset. That covers design canvases (tldraw, Excalidraw, Figma), GIS (ArcGIS, QGIS, Mapbox) and professional lighting consoles (grandMA3, ETC Eos, Avolites Titan, Sidus Link Pro). The set-visualization literature explains why: when members and non-members are interleaved, region shapes enclose non-members, break into fragments or twist into tortuous outlines, and the authors of those techniques recommend falling back to per-item marks. Each of vibes' three abandoned experiments (the convex-hull rope, the ringed cut-outs and the Voronoi territories) reproduces one of these documented failures, so reverting to rings is the right call. The current ring, though, is the weakest kind of per-item mark. It is a 1.5 px warm-white (`#fffaf2`) stroke drawn exactly where the simulated floor is brightest, in nearly the color of a warm bulb at full. By the WCAG formula it reaches only about 1.0–1.4:1 against bright warm floor tones, well below the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks of state indicators. In the closest controlled experiment, a plain ring around dots in a multicolored scene was the technique users missed most often and rated hardest. The fix stays per-item:

- a two-tone casing (a dark stroke and a light stroke together) at constant screen width, drawn on top;
- a hover style that differs from the selected style;
- a brief motion transient when the selection changes;
- all/some/none states on group and tag chips instead of shapes on the plan;
- a physical "identify" pulse on the real lights.

On touch, vibes' nearest-light hit test and its tap-to-toggle mode already match platform and research practice. The remaining gap is choosing between several bulbs that all sit under one fingertip.

## Canvases, maps and consoles all mark the item, not the group

Design tools give every selected object its own thin outline and size it in screen pixels, not artwork units. tldraw loops over the selected shapes and strokes each outline in `hsl(214, 84%, 56%)` at `1.5 / zoom` px. Hover adds the hovered shape to the same list with the same stroke. Hover indicators are suppressed entirely on coarse (touch) pointers, and drop or bind targets get a thicker 2 px stroke ([tldraw ShapeIndicatorOverlayUtil](https://github.com/tldraw/tldraw/blob/main/packages/tldraw/src/lib/overlays/ShapeIndicatorOverlayUtil.ts)). Excalidraw draws a 1 px violet (`#6965db`) border on each selected element, **padded 4 screen px away from the element's edge**. It adds a dashed common bounding box only to hold the transform handles, and it uses dashing to mean "group", "locked" or "another collaborator's selection" ([Excalidraw interactiveScene.ts](https://github.com/excalidraw/excalidraw/blob/master/packages/excalidraw/renderer/interactiveScene.ts)). Figma's help center says a multi-selection puts "a blue box … around each object" ([Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040449873-Select-layers-and-objects)). None of these tools dims unselected objects. They rely on a hue that rarely appears in artwork, a constant-width stroke and a gap between the stroke and the object.

GIS adds one convention worth copying: **hover and selection get different colors**. The ArcGIS Maps SDK ships a `default` highlight of a cyan halo at opacity 1 with a 0.25 fill, and a separate yellow `temporary` preset meant for hovering ([ArcGIS HighlightOptions](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@arcgis/core/views/support/HighlightOptions.d.ts)). QGIS defaults to yellow and lets each layer substitute an entirely different selection symbol ([PyQGIS QgsVectorLayerSelectionProperties](https://qgis.org/pyqgis/master/core/QgsVectorLayerSelectionProperties.html)). Consumer maps restyle by state instead: Mapbox Standard has a configurable blue `select` color ([Mapbox iOS](https://docs.mapbox.com/ios/maps/examples/standard-style-interactions-ui-kit/)), and Apple's selected marker glyph is documented at roughly twice the normal size ([Titanium MapKit wrapper](https://titaniumsdk.com/api/modules/map/annotation.html), a secondary source). The GIS colors assume a dark or mid-tone basemap. By the WCAG luminance formula, cyan reaches only about 1.25:1 against white and yellow about 1.07:1. Users moving from ArcMap to ArcGIS Pro complain that without the old cyan halo, "changing the selection color alone" leaves selections hard to see ([Esri Community](https://community.esri.com/t5/mining-questions/selection-halos-for-arcpro/td-p/246238)). That is a warning that hue alone is not enough.

Lighting consoles are the closest precedent, and they split into two families. Onyx and ETC Mosaic **recolor the fixture**: Onyx draws selected fixtures bright green and the last-selected one red ([Onyx manual](https://support.obsidiancontrol.com/Content/Onyx_Manual/Programming/Fixtures_and_Groups/Selecting_Fixtures.htm)), and Mosaic defaults to red ([Mosaic Designer help](https://www.etcconnect.com/webdocs/controls/mosaicdesigner/Content/Help/reference/layout/selecting%20fixtures.htm)). That only works when the glyph is not also showing live output, because a green "selected" fixture is indistinguishable from a green light. The consoles surveyed that draw fixtures in their live color all **keep the body in live color and put selection on a separate layer**. grandMA3's Layout View "displays the selected fixtures in a yellow frame" ([grandMA3 Layout View Settings](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA3/2.5/HTML/layout_view_settings.html)). ETC Magic Sheet symbols carry two tagged SVG parts: `etc_symbol_base` links to channel color and intensity, and `etc_symbol_outline` shows selection. When the outline is too faint, ETC's advice is to "add another solid rectangle to the outline layer to show the selection better" ([ETC Support](https://support.etcconnect.com/ETC/Consoles/Eos_Family/Software_and_Programming/Custom_Magic_Sheet_Fixture_Symbols)). In other words, make the mark bigger; don't recolor the fill. Avolites Titan puts a cyan bar at the bottom of a live-color tile ([Avolites Layouts](https://manual.avolites.com/docs/controlling-fixtures/layouts)). Sidus Link Pro mirrors each light's color in its cell and adds selection-order numbers and status dots ([Sidus Link Pro help](https://help.aputure.com/en/sidus-link-pro/layout-view)).

Group membership in consoles lives on buttons, never as shapes on the plot. Eos group buttons whose channels are only partly in the selection appear "partially highlighted" ([ETC Eos L2 Workbook](https://www.etcconnect.com/uploadedFiles/Main_Site/Documents/Public/Video_Tutorial/Eos_Family_L2_Enhanced_v3.2.pdf)). grandMA3 group elements color themselves with a "For All" or "For Some" color ([grandMA3 Edit Layout Elements](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA3/2.5/HTML/layout_elements_edit.html)). Only two hull-like shapes turned up anywhere in the research:

- grandMA3's Selection Grid draws a green outline around the overall selection, but in an abstract grid view, not on the stage plot ([grandMA3 Selection](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA3/2.5/HTML/operate_selection.html)).
- Leaflet.markercluster shows a convex hull on hover, but only to preview a cluster's extent ([Leaflet.markercluster README](https://github.com/Leaflet/Leaflet.markercluster/blob/master/README.md)).

Consoles answer "which light is that?" in the room, not on screen. grandMA3's Highlight sends selected fixtures to full, open white, Lowlight sends a sub-selection to about 20% dark blue, and Solo forces non-selected fixtures to zero; Next steps through the selection one fixture at a time ([grandMA3 Highlight, Lowlight and Solo](https://help.malighting.com/grandMA3/2.5/HTML/highlight_lowlight_solo.html)). Sidus Link Pro's Find Fixture makes each light "subtly flash four times" in turn ([Sidus Link Pro help](https://help.aputure.com/en/sidus-link-pro/layout-view)), and Hue lamps "flash shortly in the last set color" when identified ([deCONZ issue #183](https://github.com/dresden-elektronik/deconz-rest-plugin/issues/183)). Pro tools dim the real rig, not the picture of it. A search-result summary of an ETC feature request also reports a user asking to turn off Augment3d's yellow selection highlight because it hides the true look ([ETC Community](https://community.etcconnect.com/control_consoles/eos-family-consoles/i/feature-requests/how-to-disable-selected-highlight_in_yellow-function-in-augment3d)). The page itself could not be read.

## Each abandoned group shape reproduces a published failure mode

Every controlled set-visualization study tests three to 26 sets, never one selected set against everything else, so no experiment settles vibes' case directly. The literature does, however, catalogue exactly how region overlays break when members and non-members are interleaved. vibes' three experiments each hit a different entry in that catalogue.

**The convex-hull rope is false inclusion.** The LineSets authors warn that on a map a hull "covers a larger region than necessary" and erroneously appears to contain other items ([Alper et al. 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2011/12/linesets_infovis2011.pdf)). In their 12-person study, **Bubble Sets managed only 66.7% accuracy on membership questions versus 88.9% for LineSets**, and 11 of 12 participants preferred LineSets. KelpFusion turned the lesson into a construction rule: never fill a face that contains a non-member, because doing so "incorrectly implies" that the non-member belongs. In its 13-person study of Boston restaurants, Bubble Sets scored **54.5% overall versus 86.5% for KelpFusion** ([Meulemans et al. 2013](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/kelpfusion_tvcg2013.pdf)). On a rail where color and old bulbs alternate, any hull around the selected color bulbs encloses every old bulb between them.

**The ringed cut-outs are the "snake around non-members" outcome.** Bubble Sets "may still appear to include elements from other sets" even when it tries to exclude them ([Alper et al. 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2011/12/linesets_infovis2011.pdf)). SimpleSets, the most recent design answer, explicitly prefers stacking orders that avoid "cut-outs" around other points ([van den Broek et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14433v1)). With strict alternation, a cut-out shape is half holes. KelpFusion's other rule also applies: large filled faces "obscure much of a background map, are too visually dominant, and may suggest a higher density" ([Meulemans et al. 2013](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/kelpfusion_tvcg2013.pdf)). In vibes the background is the simulated light on the floor, which is the very thing the user is there to judge.

**The Voronoi territories are GMap's fragmentation.** In Jianu et al.'s roughly 800-person study, GMap's Voronoi "countries" were the best technique when groups were cohesive. They **performed significantly worse (p<0.001) when same-group nodes were spatially scattered**, because the disjoint pieces have "no explicit connection." A hand-edited version that linked the fragments fixed this ([Jianu et al. 2014](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/15379/1/paper_TVCG2014_groupinfo.pdf)). Alternating bulbs are the worst case for fragmentation: every selected cell borders an unselected one. Forcing the territories to be contiguous does not rescue the approach. MapSets' authors concede interleaved inputs need "'octopus'-like regions" and recommend relaxing the constraint ([Efrat et al. 2015](https://jgaa.info/index.php/jgaa/article/download/paper364/2581)).

Lines are no escape either. The survey literature warns that line overlays "interfere with the base visualization if it already uses lines" ([Alsallakh et al. 2016](https://www.cvast.tuwien.ac.at/sites/default/files/bibcite/459/publik_262247.pdf)). Jianu found LineSets curves misread as connections ([Jianu et al. 2014](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/15379/1/paper_TVCG2014_groupinfo.pdf)), and KelpFusion participants read them as implying an order that didn't exist ([Meulemans et al. 2013](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/kelpfusion_tvcg2013.pdf)). vibes' plan is walls and rails, which are all lines.

The strongest case for regions deserves a fair hearing. In Jianu's study, BubbleSets was the most accurate technique for group membership and plain colored nodes were the worst. Regions earn that by offering large, continuous color areas that peripheral vision can follow. Colored nodes "showed very poor performance" when clusters were interspersed ([Jianu et al. 2014](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/15379/1/paper_TVCG2014_groupinfo.pdf)). But Jianu attributes most of those errors to **similar hues assigned to 26 different groups**. A single selection drawn in one reserved style against everything else removes that confound. The KelpFusion authors themselves concede Bubble Sets "may remain a good choice" when sets rarely overlap. Left untrained, half of LineSets' participants marked membership with "only color" on each item ([Alper et al. 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2011/12/linesets_infovis2011.pdf)). The survey's verdict fits vibes: when sets need not be shown "as a connected object," per-element glyphs are the "lightweight alternative" ([Alsallakh et al. 2016](https://www.cvast.tuwien.ac.at/sites/default/files/bibcite/459/publik_262247.pdf)). Per-item marks do lose the area that made regions easy to see. The design task is to give that area back to each item.

## The current ring vanishes exactly where the floor is brightest

In `src/web/components/RoomMap.tsx`, each selected fixture gets a 1.5 px `#fffaf2` stroke 5 px outside its edge. Hover uses the same color at 45% alpha. Two things sit right next to that ring. The lit floor is brightest directly beneath each light, because the floor is a tone-mapped simulation of the lights' own output. A lit fixture's fill is "pushed toward white as it brightens." So the ring, the fixture and the floor share one hue family and, at full brightness, nearly one luminance. That is Ware's "like interferes with like" in its purest form. In Mairena et al.'s distractor study, adding distractor variety "only reduced the effectiveness of visual variables that were similar to the distractors" ([Mairena et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8841630/)). Healey's review ranks luminance above hue in salience ([Healey](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/healey/PP/)). Strobelt et al. found color pops out only "if the background does not vary with respect to color" ([Strobelt et al. 2016](https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/20160101-guidelines-for-effective-usage-of-text-highlighting-techniques/paper)).

The arithmetic makes the problem concrete. WCAG 1.4.11 requires the visual information that identifies a selected state to reach **3:1 against adjacent colors** ([W3C Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). For a ring on this map, the adjacent colors are the floor and the fixture. The table below applies the WCAG luminance formula (my calculation) to sample floor tones running from a bright warm pool to the dark void:

| Floor tone under the ring | Current warm-white ring `#fffaf2` | Near-black `#141210` | Canvas-tool blue `#3182ed` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot spot, `#fff1d6` | **1.07:1** | 16.7:1 | 3.4:1 |
| Bright warm pool, `#f2d2a0` | **1.39:1** | 12.9:1 | 2.6:1 |
| Mid amber, `#d9a86a` | **2.07:1** | 8.7:1 | 1.8:1 |
| Dim brown, `#8a6a40` | 4.8:1 | 3.8:1 | 1.3:1 |
| Unlit floor, `#3a2a1a` | 13.2:1 | **1.36:1** | 3.6:1 |

No single color clears 3:1 across the floor's range. The warm white fails wherever there is light, the dark fails in the void, and even the saturated mid-blue that design tools favor fails on amber. **A pair of strokes, one dark and one light, always puts at least one edge above 3:1**, which is why cartographers use halos: "by making the text dark and the halo light (or vice versa), you can ensure that text elements always have enough contrast" ([EU Data Visualisation Guide](https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/text-halos)). Hue matters less than consistency. Griffin and Robinson found no significant difference between red and black map highlights, and conclude that what counts is reserving a channel for highlighting ([Griffin & Robinson 2015](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/comparing-color-and-leader-line-highlighting-strategies-in-coordi/)).

Width matters too. In Waldner et al.'s scene of 1,000 colored dots, the "halo" condition was a ring twice the dot's radius with no other change to the scene. It "was more likely to be missed than a flickering target or a bright halo in a dark scene, and also led to the slowest reaction times," and "all users stated that halo was the hardest technique" ([Waldner et al. 2014](https://vis.cs.ucdavis.edu/vis2014papers/TVCG/papers/2456_20tvcg12-waldner-2346352.pdf)). In Doerr et al.'s situated study, outlines came third of four techniques because they were hard to perceive "due to small size and insufficient contrast" ([Doerr et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.15321v3)). Borders do work when they add footprint. In Strobelt's text study a border ranked among the top highlights, and the authors attribute this to making the target "appear bigger" ([Strobelt et al. 2016](https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/20160101-guidelines-for-effective-usage-of-text-highlighting-techniques/paper)). Szafir showed that the color difference needed to tell marks apart grows as marks shrink, and grows further among distractor points ([Szafir VIS 2017 slides](https://cmci.colorado.edu/visualab/VisColors/17-VIS-ModelingColorDifference.pdf)). A thin ring around a 5 px dot sits at the bad end of every one of these curves.

Dimming the unselected fixtures, the obvious alternative, is the wrong default here. Opacity-based "lowlighting" peaked at **0.56 accuracy** on Mairena's map-like hexbin display ([Mairena et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8841630/)). Fading fixtures over a bright floor lets the floor show through, so the one cue left is "selected fixture versus floor," which is exactly the contrast the floor's variation destroys. Graying out non-selected items also discards context ([Lu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05368)). In a lighting app the dimmed fixture would also misreport its real output. Waldner's best condition, "spotlight," combined a bright ring with darkening the whole scene by 75%. It won, but users called it "too obtrusive" ([Waldner et al. 2014](https://vis.cs.ucdavis.edu/vis2014papers/TVCG/papers/2456_20tvcg12-waldner-2346352.pdf)). That fits a temporary "solo" view, not the resting state.

Motion is the one channel that held up everywhere. Motion×Distance reached **0.95 accuracy** in Mairena's baseline study and stayed consistent across all five display types, including the map-like ones ([Mairena et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8841630/)). Gutwin et al. found motion "a strong popout stimulus, even at low intensities and wide angles" ([Gutwin et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3025453.3025984)). Its cost is annoyance. Waldner's resolution is a two-stage signal: an orientation burst of at most 500 ms, then a static or very gentle (<2 Hz) state ([Waldner et al. 2014](https://vis.cs.ucdavis.edu/vis2014papers/TVCG/papers/2456_20tvcg12-waldner-2346352.pdf)). Two more results argue for a persistent, uniform style:

- People can track only about four changing objects, up to eight if slow ([Alvarez & Franconeri 2007](https://visualcognition.psych.northwestern.edu/publications/AlvarezTracking2007.pdf)). A 30-light selection therefore has to be re-findable from the marks themselves, not from memory.
- Search for a known target stayed above 95% accurate regardless of color variety, while search for an unknown odd-one-out collapsed in random layouts ([Haroz & Whitney 2012](https://whitneylab.berkeley.edu/PDFs/Haroz_Whitney_2012_InfoVis.pdf)). One consistent "selected" glyph turns finding selected lights into known-target search.

## Fingers cover several bulbs, so the mark can't do the work alone

At overview zoom, vibes' bulbs fall far below every touch guideline. The minimum drawn radius is 2.5 px. My estimate is that a 5 px dot is under a millimeter across on a phone, and its 15 px ring is about 2.4 mm. Parhi et al. recommend **9.2–9.6 mm** targets for thumbs ([Parhi et al. 2006](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=155150)). Material asks for 48 dp, about 9 mm, and lets the touch target extend beyond the visible icon ([Material spacing](https://material.io/design/layout/spacing-methods.html)). Vogel and Baudisch measured **81% errors** for bare fingertip taps on 2.6 mm targets ([Vogel & Baudisch 2007](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/papers/dvogel_CHI2007.pdf)). vibes already does the important thing: on touch, a tap goes to the nearest light within at least 25 px. That is the bubble cursor's nearest-target capture with a maximum radius, which beat ordinary pointing across layout densities ([Grossman & Balakrishnan 2005](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/papers/tgrossman_CHI2005.pdf)).

What nearest-wins cannot do is say which bulb it picked when several lie inside one fingertip, as they do on a tightly spaced rail. Holz and Baudisch attribute 67% of "fat finger" inaccuracy to systematic per-user and per-posture offsets ([Holz & Baudisch 2010](https://siplab.org/projects/Generalized_Perceived_Input_Point_Model)), so the bulb nearest the contact point is not reliably the one the user meant. The closest study to vibes' task is Gunn et al.: one-handed selection of four targets among distractors on a map, with 14 px targets.

| Technique (Gunn et al. 2009) | Mean time | Error rate |
|---|---|---|
| Burst (a ring of enlarged copies of the 6 nearest targets around the thumb) | 5.2 s | **7%** |
| ZoomTap (open a zoomed view, then tap to toggle) | **4.2 s** | 41.9% |
| Shift (one target at a time through a callout) | 9.5 s | 55.9% |

Both winners avoid precise aiming ([Gunn et al. 2009](https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/assets/publication_files/2009-CHI-Gunn-OneHandedMultipleTargetTechniques.pdf)). Map SDKs solve the same problem by fanning out overlapping markers. OverlappingMarkerSpiderfier groups markers within 20 px into a circle (a spiral at 9 or more), and its `keepSpiderfied` option leaves the fan open so several picks can be made in a row ([OverlappingMarkerSpiderfier](https://github.com/MITREDan/OverlappingMarkerSpiderfier-Leaflet)). Shift contributes two details worth borrowing for any press-and-release gesture. First, commit on lift-off and discard the jitter just before it. Second, users "always used the callout for visual feedback, even if they realized that the target in the main display was visible" ([Vogel & Baudisch 2007](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/papers/dvogel_CHI2007.pdf)).

vibes' selection mode already follows platform convention. After the first selection, taps toggle lights, which matches Android's model where long-press starts selection and taps add items ([Android selection pattern](https://stuff.mit.edu/afs/sipb/project/android/docs/design/patterns/selection.html)). Long-press-then-drag draws a box, as in iPad Freeform ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-kw/guide/ipad/ipad0b4f22b0/ipados)). A double tap selects a whole rail, an analog of MagicQ's row and column selection ([MagicQ manual](https://docs.chamsys.co.uk/magicq/1.9.9.x/manual/outputs_windows.html)). A mark drawn on the fixture is hidden under the finger, so the reliable confirmation is a count in a fixed spot, which the selection chip already provides. The interleaving itself also has a structural fix. Alternation comes from fixture type, so a Figma-style "select all with same" command ([Figma Help](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040449873-Select-layers-and-objects)), such as "only the color bulbs on this rail," replaces tapping every other dot.

## Ranked recommendations for vibes

| Rank | Change | Why | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Replace the single warm-white stroke with a **two-tone casing**: a near-black stroke (~2 px) just outside a light stroke (~1.5 px), with a 2–3 px gap from the fixture's edge, sized in screen px and drawn after every fixture. At overview zoom, keep a bulb's outer edge at 2× the dot's radius or more (Waldner's ring size); panels keep the rounded-rect offset. | It is the only treatment that clears 3:1 on both the lit pools and the dark void, and it adds the footprint that makes borders work. Draw order follows Oelke's "last color will be the most salient" ([Oelke et al. 2011](https://bib.dbvis.de/uploadedFiles/320.pdf)). | Strong convergence (WCAG, cartographic halos, console outline layers). The casing itself is untested for point marks. |
| 2 | Give hover a **different form**, not just lower alpha: a single thin light stroke with no dark casing, or a dashed one. Suppress hover on coarse pointers. | Hover and selection carry different colors in ArcGIS and different weights in tldraw. Today a hovered unselected bulb looks like a faint selected one. | GIS and canvas-tool convention |
| 3 | Add a **≤400 ms transient** when lights join the selection: each ring starts at about 2× radius and contracts onto the fixture, then stays static. Skip it under `prefers-reduced-motion`. | Motion is the only channel that survived clutter and map backgrounds, and a transient defeats change blindness without lasting annoyance. | Strong for detection (Mairena, Waldner). Untested on multi-target selections. |
| 4 | Show **all / some / none** on tag, rail and group chips. Pressing or hovering a chip briefly pulses its members' rings instead of drawing a shape. | This is where consoles put group state (Eos partial highlight, MA For All/For Some), and it avoids every region failure mode. | Industry practice |
| 5 | **Touch disambiguation**: when two or more lights fall inside finger reach, a long-press opens a Burst-style ring of up to 6 enlarged copies that stays open for toggling, committing on lift-off. Add "select same type on this rail." | It attacks the one touch failure vibes still has, and alternation is a type pattern. | Gunn et al. (7% errors); OMS practice |
| 6 | Add an **Identify** action: selected color bulbs breathe in their current color, old on/off bulbs blink, and an optional Next/Back steps through them one at a time. | It is the industry's real answer to "which light is this?", and it keeps the on-screen fixtures truthful. | Universal in consoles and consumer systems |
| 7 | At most, an opt-in **Solo view** that darkens everything except the selection while held. Never dim by default, recolor fixtures or draw hulls, territories or connecting lines. | Spotlight works but is obtrusive. Dimming alone fails on bright maps. Recoloring collides with live color. Regions and lines fail on interleaving and on line-drawn plans. | Mixed (Waldner, Mairena, set-vis studies) |

If a grouping cue is ever wanted again, the only defensible form is KelpFusion's rule applied to rails. Draw a capsule only around a **run of consecutive selected bulbs on one rail with zero unselected bulbs inside it**, which is SimpleSets' "bank" pattern ([van den Broek et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.14433v1)), and keep the per-bulb casings underneath. Strict alternation produces no such runs, so the capsule appears only where it is honest.

## Conclusion

The group shapes were not a wrong idea about grouping. They were a sound instinct about visibility, pursued with the wrong tool. The experiments that favored regions credit them with more colored area and continuous contrast, and that is exactly what the thin warm ring lacks. The hull, cut-out and Voronoi versions bought that area by claiming floor that belongs to unselected bulbs. A per-item casing gets the same area and contrast on territory each fixture already owns. The deeper lesson is specific to vibes: the background is a rendering of the lights themselves, so any selection color defined relative to the scene will collide with some light somewhere. Only a luminance pair, light against dark, holds everywhere on the floor.

The evidence has real limits. No published study tests many selected marks among many unselected ones over a bright, emissive, chromatically varying background, and the two-tone casing for point symbols has practitioner backing but no measured effect size. That gap is small enough to close in the app itself. A quick check is worthwhile: count the selected lights on a fully lit floor with the current ring, then with the casing, and time each.
